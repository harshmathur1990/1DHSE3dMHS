\section{What the mode does}
Given the temperature $T(x,y,z)$ of a solar atmosphere on a geometric-height grid, the mode computes the gas pressure
$P$, the density $\rho$, the electron density and the optical depth $\tau_{500}$ that every vertical column would have
if it were in \textbf{hydrostatic equilibrium on its own}. Each column is independent of its neighbours: no horizontal
force balance is attempted and the magnetic field plays no role. The result is the non-magnetic product of the code,
the reference against which the magnetic modes are measured, and the starting state of \code{mhs-poisson-z} and
\code{full-mhs-z}.

\section{The equation}
In a static, non-magnetic atmosphere the vertical force balance on a gas parcel is
\[
\frac{dP}{dz}=-\rho g .
\]
With $\rho=\rho(T,P)$ and $T(z)$ given, this is an ordinary differential equation along each column. Written for
$u=\ln P$,
\[
\boxed{\;\frac{du}{dz}=-\frac{g\,\rho\big(T(z),e^{u}\big)}{e^{u}}=-\frac{1}{H(T,P)}\;}
\]
For an ideal gas of fixed composition and ionisation, $H$ depends on $T$ only and the solution is the familiar
exponential atmosphere $P(z)=P_{\rm ref}\exp\!\big(-\int dz/H\big)$. In the Sun $\bar\mu$ changes with ionisation and
molecule formation, so $H$ depends on $P$ too; that is why the equation is integrated numerically with a full EOS. In
the photosphere $H\approx100$--150\,km; over the 3.5\,Mm of the production box the pressure falls by about eight orders of
magnitude (median $10^{5.0}$\,Pa at the bottom, $10^{-3.1}$\,Pa at the top, with a spread of 5.5\,dex between columns
there), which is why $u=\ln P$ rather than $P$ is integrated.

The column mass is integrated along with $u$: $dm/dz=-\rho$, so that $P(z)-P_{\rm top}=g\,m(z)$ can be checked.

\section{Where the pressure is fixed (the anchor)}
A first-order equation needs one value per column. The default is a \textbf{bottom anchor}: the same pressure
$P_{\rm ref}$ at the lowest grid level in every column, and integration upward. For the production input
(\code{IO/input-mhs-hse.nc}, Bifrost \code{en024048\_hion} snapshot 385 cropped to $-0.49\ldots+2.98$\,Mm)
$P_{\rm ref}=1.035\times10^5$\,Pa, the median Bifrost pressure on that plane (spread 0.08\,dex). Alternatives are a top
anchor (default 0.1\,Pa) and an anchor at a chosen height $z_{\rm ref}$ (integrated both ways).

Why the bottom: the deep layers are where the pressure is best known and least sensitive to the details above. A top
anchor makes the photospheric pressure depend on the full chromospheric and transition-region temperature structure,
which differs strongly from column to column.

\section{Equation of state and opacity}
$\rho$, $n_e$ and $\chi_{500}$ come from a table built with Wittmann's LTE equation of state (solar composition,
H$_2$ included, a Saha branch for H and He above a switch temperature) and the continuum opacity at 5000\,\AA,
interpolated bicubically in $(\ln T,\ln P)$. The table \code{cache/eos\_table\_fullz.h5} covers 2172\,K--1.78\,MK and
$10^{-13}$--$10^{15}$\,Pa. One EOS state supplies $\rho$, $n_e$, the electron pressure and $\chi_{500}$, so they are
consistent with each other.

\section{Optical depth}
With $P(z)$ and $T(z)$ known, $\chi_{500}$ is known at every level and
\[
\tau_{500}(z)=\tau_{\rm top}+\int_{z}^{z_{\rm top}}\chi_{500}\,dz' ,
\]
integrated downward with the trapezoid rule. $\tau_{\rm top}$ accounts for the gas above the box. The default closure
(\code{exp\_tail}) assumes that $\chi$ keeps falling exponentially with the scale height of the top two levels,
$H_\chi=(z_1-z_2)/\ln(\chi_2/\chi_1)$, giving $\tau_{\rm top}=\chi_1H_\chi$. Where that is not meaningful ($\chi$ not
falling, or $H_\chi>5000$\,km) half a cell, $\tfrac12\chi_1(z_1-z_2)$, is used and the column is flagged. The height of
$\tau_{500}=1$, $z_{\tau1}(x,y)$, is the ``visible surface'': spectral lines form at fixed $\tau$, so this is what an
inversion sees.

\section{What the result means, and what it does not}
\begin{itemize}
\item Each column is exactly hydrostatic. \textbf{Horizontally the atmosphere is not in balance}: neighbouring columns
with different temperatures have different $P(z)$, so there are horizontal pressure gradients that nothing balances.
The mode reports them as the horizontal force residual $R_x,R_y$ (with $B=0$), and labels the output
\code{vertical\_hse\_only}.
\item A non-magnetic atmosphere can be in full 3-D static balance only if it is horizontally uniform (the curl of the
force balance requires $\partial_x\rho=\partial_y\rho=0$). A structured atmosphere in true 3-D balance needs the
Lorentz force, which is what the \code{mhs-*} and \code{full-mhs-z} modes add.
\item The temperature is never altered.
\end{itemize}

\section{Typical result}
Production input, full cube ($504\times504\times177$): $z_{\tau1}$ has percentiles 1/50/99 of $-29$ / $64$ / $127$\,km.
The column-to-column scatter of $z_{\tau1}$ is set entirely by the temperature structure, because every column carries
the same bottom pressure.
