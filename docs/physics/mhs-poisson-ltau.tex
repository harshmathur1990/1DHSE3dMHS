\doctitle{mhs-poisson-ltau: magnetohydrostatic correction on an optical-depth grid}{Physics and method of \code{run.mode = "mhs-poisson-ltau"} in HSEMHS. A self-contained description.}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent An inversion of solar spectra gives the temperature and the magnetic field on surfaces of constant optical depth.
This mode computes the gas pressure on every optical-depth surface \emph{and} the geometric height of every surface in every
pixel column such that pressure, gravity and the Lorentz force of the given field balance as well as possible in all three
directions. On an optical-depth grid the two cannot be separated: the heights of the surfaces follow from the opacity, which
depends on the pressure. We derive the vertical and horizontal force balance on tilted optical-depth surfaces, the Lorentz
force in these curvilinear coordinates, and the least-squares objective; we then describe the Gauss--Newton
(Levenberg--Marquardt) iteration with its exact linearisation, the preconditioner, the step control and the stopping rule.
We discuss what fixes the pressure level (no external reference pressure is used), what fixes the heights, and which
quantities remain undetermined. All assumptions are listed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A spectral line inversion describes the atmosphere in each pixel as a function of the continuum optical depth at 500\,nm,
$\tau_{500}$: it gives $T$ and $\B$ at, e.g., $\log_{10}\tau_{500}=-8.0,-7.9,\dots,+1.4$. The simplest way to give such an
atmosphere a pressure and a geometry is to put every pixel column into hydrostatic equilibrium on its own (the mode
\code{column-hse-ltau}). The columns are then not in horizontal force balance with each other, and the Lorentz force of the
field is ignored. This mode goes further, in the spirit of a magnetohydrostatic ``Poisson step'' on a geometric grid: $T$ and
$\B$ on the $\tau$ surfaces stay fixed, and we look for the pressure on every $\tau$ surface and the height of every $\tau$
surface in every column that together balance the forces best.

\textbf{Input:} the $\log_{10}\tau_{500}$ levels; the horizontal coordinates $x$, $y$ (periodic box); $T$, $B_x$, $B_y$, $B_z$
on every level of every column; the gravity $g$. \textbf{Output:} on the same $\tau$ levels, $P$, $\rho$ and the height $z$;
the height of $\tau_{500}=1$ per column. $T$ and $\B$ are copied unchanged.

\section{The force balance}\label{sec:force}
A static gas has zero net force per unit volume:
\begin{equation}
\F=-\nabla P+\J\times\B-\rho g\,\zh=0 ,\qquad
\J=\frac{\nabla\times\B}{\mu_0},\qquad
\J\times\B=-\nabla\!\left(\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}\right)+\frac{(\B\cdot\nabla)\B}{\mu_0}.\label{eq:F}
\end{equation}
The Lorentz force is a magnetic pressure plus a tension along curved field lines. With the field fixed this balance can in
general not be satisfied exactly. Taking the curl of Eq.~\eqref{eq:F} removes the pressure and leaves
$\nabla\times(\J\times\B)=g\,(\partial_y\rho,-\partial_x\rho,0)$: only the tension of the field can hold up horizontal
density differences, and a field taken from an inversion has no reason to have exactly the right tension. We therefore look
for the state that balances the forces \emph{as well as possible} (Sect.~\ref{sec:obj}).

The new difficulty here is that all derivatives in $\F$ are taken in physical space, at fixed height, whereas the data live
on $\tau$ surfaces whose heights are themselves unknown.

\section{Optical depth, heights and mass}\label{sec:geom}
\subsection{Heights follow from the opacity}
Going down by $dz$ adds the optical depth $d\tau=-\chi\,dz$. With $s=\ln\tau$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{dz}{ds}=-\frac{\tau}{\chi(T,P)}=-a ,\qquad
z_{k+1}=z_k-\int_{s_k}^{s_{k+1}}a\,ds\approx z_k-\frac{\Delta s_k}{2}\,(a_k+a_{k+1}) .\label{eq:height}
\end{equation}
The height of a level is the height of the level above it minus the thickness of the layer in between, and the thickness is
set by the opacity, which depends on the pressure. In a column, the heights below the cut level $k_c$ are therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;z_{k_c}=c,\qquad z_k=c-\sum_{m=k_c}^{k-1}\frac{\Delta s_m}{2}\,(a_m+a_{m+1}),\quad k>k_c\;}\label{eq:zsum}
\end{equation}
Two consequences: \emph{a change of pressure at one level moves every level below it} (higher pressure, more opacity, a
thinner layer, everything below shifts up); and the unknowns of a column can be taken to be the pressures on its levels plus
one height $c$. All other heights follow. Equation~\eqref{eq:zsum} is a cumulative sum: a lower-triangular linear operator
acting down the column.

\subsection{Optical depth measures mass}
Since $\chi=\kappa\rho$ with $\kappa$ the extinction per unit mass and $dm=-\rho\,dz$ the mass added going down,
$d\tau=\kappa\,dm$: optical depth is column mass weighted by the opacity. This is why, unlike on a geometric grid, the
pressure on a $\tau$ grid needs no external reference value (Sect.~\ref{sec:level}).

\section{\texorpdfstring{Vertical balance along $\tau$}{Vertical balance along tau}}\label{sec:vert}
The vertical component of Eq.~\eqref{eq:F} is $\partial P/\partial z=-(\rho g-L_z)$: the pressure difference across a slab
carries its weight minus what the vertical Lorentz force carries. Divide by $dz=-a\,ds$ and by $P$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\frac{d\ln P}{d\ln\tau}=\frac{(\rho g-L_z)\,\tau}{\chi\,P}=W\,a\;},\qquad W\equiv\frac{\rho g-L_z}{P}=g\,q-L_z\,e^{-u}.
\label{eq:vert}
\end{equation}
Without the field this is the hydrostatic equation in optical depth, $dP/d\tau=g/\kappa$. An upward Lorentz force
($L_z>0$) carries part of the weight, so the pressure rises less steeply with optical depth.

\section{Horizontal balance on tilted surfaces}\label{sec:horiz}
A $\tau$ surface is generally not flat: its height $z(x,y,\tau)$ varies across the box. Consider the pressure along such a
surface, $\tilde P(x,\tau)=P\big(x,z(x,\tau)\big)$. By the chain rule,
\begin{equation}
\left.\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}\right|_\tau=\left.\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}\right|_z+\frac{\partial P}{\partial z}\left.\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right|_\tau .
\end{equation}
The physical force needs $\partial P/\partial x$ at fixed height. Solving for it and using the vertical balance
$\partial P/\partial z=-(\rho g-L_z)$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;F_x=-\left.\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}\right|_\tau-(\rho g-L_z)\left.\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right|_\tau+L_x,\qquad
F_y=-\left.\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right|_\tau-(\rho g-L_z)\left.\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\right|_\tau+L_y\;}\label{eq:horiz}
\end{equation}
The middle term has a simple meaning. If a $\tau$ surface slopes, comparing pressures along it means comparing points at
different heights, and the weight of the gas between them must be subtracted: on a tilted surface part of the weight acts
sideways. Horizontal balance therefore ties together the pressures \emph{and} the heights of neighbouring columns: a pressure
difference along a $\tau$ surface can be balanced by a tilt of the surface.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The Lorentz force on a $\tau$ grid}{The Lorentz force on a tau grid}}\label{sec:lorentz}
$\J=\nabla\times\B/\mu_0$ needs derivatives in physical space. With the field given on the $\tau$ levels, the same chain rule
gives, for any field component $f$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}=\frac{\partial f/\partial s}{\partial z/\partial s},\qquad
\left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right|_z=\left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right|_\tau-\left.\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right|_\tau\frac{\partial f}{\partial z},\qquad
\left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right|_z=\left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right|_\tau-\left.\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\right|_\tau\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}.
\label{eq:curvi}
\end{equation}
Then $\J=\mu_0^{-1}(\partial_yB_z-\partial_zB_y,\ \partial_zB_x-\partial_xB_z,\ \partial_xB_y-\partial_yB_x)$ and
$\Lv=\J\times\B$. So even though the field values on the $\tau$ levels are fixed, the currents and the Lorentz force depend on
where the levels lie: if two levels move apart, the vertical gradients of the field between them become smaller, and so do the
currents they imply. The Lorentz force is therefore recomputed whenever the heights change, and the solver takes its
dependence on the heights into account (Sect.~\ref{sec:lin}).

\section{The quantity minimised}\label{sec:obj}
\subsection{Relative residuals}
As on a geometric grid, the forces are divided by the pressure, so that lowering the pressure is not rewarded:
\begin{equation}
R_x=\frac{F_x}{P}=-\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right|_\tau-W\left.\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right|_\tau+L_xe^{-u},\qquad
R_y=\frac{F_y}{P}\ \text{(likewise)},\qquad
R_z=\Delta u-\int W\,a\,ds .\label{eq:R}
\end{equation}
$R_x$, $R_y$ are evaluated on every solved level; $R_z$ is the vertical balance~\eqref{eq:vert} integrated over one level
interval, so it is the misfit of $\ln P$ across the interval.

\subsection{Least squares}
At every point there are three equations; per column the unknowns are $n-1$ pressures and one height (Sect.~\ref{sec:unk}).
There are more equations than unknowns and, by Sect.~\ref{sec:force}, no exact solution. The pressure and heights are
therefore those that minimise
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\Phi=\sum_{i,j}\left[\sum_{k=k_c}^{N_\tau}\overline{\Delta z}_k\,\bar H_k^2\big(R_{x,ijk}^2+R_{y,ijk}^2\big)
+\sum_{k=k_c}^{N_\tau-1}\frac{\bar H_k\bar H_{k+1}}{\overline{\Delta z}_{k+1/2}}\,R_{z,ij,k+1/2}^2\right]\;}\label{eq:Phi}
\end{equation}
$\bar H_k$ is the median over the box of the scale height $P/(\rho g)$ on level $k$ and $\overline{\Delta z}_{k+1/2}$ the
median thickness of interval $k$, both from the starting state and fixed during the solve; $\overline{\Delta z}_k$ is the mean
of the two adjacent interval thicknesses (half the single one at the end levels). Since $\bar H R_x\approx F_x/(\rho g)$ and
$\bar H R_z/\overline{\Delta z}\approx F_z/(\rho g)$, $\Phi$ approximates a volume integral (per unit area) of the squared
imbalance in units of the weight of the gas, so that the dense deep layers do not swamp the upper ones.

\section{The unknowns and what fixes the pressure level}\label{sec:unk}
\subsection{Unknowns}
In every column the solve determines
\begin{itemize}
\item the height $c_{ij}$ of the cut level $k_c$ (the level nearest to $\log_{10}\tau=-4$), and
\item $u_{ijk}=\ln P$ on the levels $k=k_c+1,\dots,N_\tau$ below the cut.
\end{itemize}
All heights below the cut follow from Eq.~\eqref{eq:zsum}. The total is $n$ unknowns per column against $3n-1$ residuals.

\subsection{Above the cut}
Above the cut the field is strong compared with the thin gas (low plasma $\beta$), and a field taken from an inversion is far
from force-free there. Balancing its Lorentz force by the pressure would require enormous pressure gradients that would
propagate down into the photosphere. Above the cut ($\log_{10}\tau<-4$) the levels are therefore not solved: their pressures and
their distances to the cut level stay those of the starting state, which is hydrostatic in $\tau$, and the whole upper part of a
column moves rigidly up and down with $c$.

\subsection{The pressure level}\label{sec:level}
This mode uses \textbf{no external reference pressure}, neither at the bottom nor anywhere else. The pressure on the cut level
is not an unknown either: it keeps its value from the starting state. That value comes from integrating the hydrostatic
equation in $\tau$, $dP/d\tau=g/\kappa$, from the top of the grid, where $P_1=g\tau_1/\kappa_1$ (the gas above the top level
assumed to have the opacity $\kappa_1$ and zero pressure at $\tau=0$). Because $\tau$ measures opacity-weighted mass, this fixes
the pressure without any outside information: at the cut level the pressure is the weight of the gas that produces the optical
depth $10^{-4}$. Below the cut the vertical residual $R_z$ links each pressure to the one above it, starting from this fixed
value; the pressures below are thus set by the force balance and the $\tau$ scale. The price is that the result depends on the
opacity: if the $\tau$ scale of the input was computed with a different opacity, the pressures differ in proportion.

\subsection{The starting state}
The iteration starts from the column-hydrostatic solution in $\tau$: each column integrated from the top with
$d\ln P/d\ln\tau=g\tau\rho/(\chi P)$ and $dz/d\ln\tau=-\tau/\chi$ (Heun substeps sized so that $\ln P$ changes by
at most about 0.05--0.1 per substep at the slopes of both ends, which resolves the steep but genuine pressure rise below
hot-over-cool interfaces; a column whose pressure leaves the equation-of-state table fails, and then no solve is
attempted), aligned so that the total pressure
$P+B^2/2\mu_0$ is the same in all columns at one height, and gauged so that the mean height of $\tau=1$ is zero. From this
state come the fixed upper levels, the cut-level pressures, the weights of $\Phi$, and the first guess of $u$ and $c$.

\section{Assumptions}\label{sec:assume}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{Static gas:} velocities neglected.
\item \textbf{$T$ and $\B$ fixed on the $\tau$ levels.} The field values travel with their levels; the Lorentz force is computed
from them at the current heights.
\item \textbf{Best compromise, not equilibrium:} the solution minimises Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi}.
\item \textbf{Pressure level from the $\tau$ scale:} the pressure on the cut level is that of the column-hydrostatic solution
(top closure $P_1=g\tau_1/\kappa_1$); no external reference pressure.
\item \textbf{Fixed upper atmosphere:} above $\log_{10}\tau=-4$ the columns are $\tau$-hydrostatic and rigid.
\item \textbf{Consistent opacity:} the input $\tau$ scale is the vertical 500\,nm continuum optical depth of the same LTE
equation of state and opacity as the table.
\item \textbf{Horizontally periodic box}, uniform horizontal spacing.
\item \textbf{Weights} from the starting state, fixed.
\item \textbf{LTE ideal gas; constant vertical gravity}; plane-parallel geometry.
\item \textbf{No condition on $\nabla\cdot\B$:} nothing enforces $\nabla\cdot\B=0$ for the field at its new positions.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The discrete problem}\label{sec:disc}
For each column $(i,j)$ and solved level $k=k_c\dots N_\tau$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{State:} $q_{ijk}=\rho/P$, $\ln\chi_{ijk}$ and $a_{ijk}=\exp(s_k-\ln\chi_{ijk})$ from the table at
$(\ln T_{ijk},u_{ijk})$.
\item \textbf{Heights:} $z_{ij,k_c}=c_{ij}$; levels above the cut at $c_{ij}$ plus their fixed offsets; levels below by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:zsum}.
\item \textbf{Field derivatives} on all levels: along the column
$(\partial_zf)_{ijk}=(f_{ij,k-1}-f_{ij,k+1})/(z_{ij,k-1}-z_{ij,k+1})$ (one-sided at the top and bottom levels); on the $\tau$
surface centred periodic differences, $(\partial_xf)|_\tau=(f_{i+1,jk}-f_{i-1,jk})/(2\Delta x)$; then Eq.~\eqref{eq:curvi}
with the surface slopes $(\partial_xz)|_\tau$, $(\partial_yz)|_\tau$ computed the same way. Then $\J$, $\Lv$.
\item \textbf{Residuals:} with $W_{ijk}=g\,q_{ijk}-(L_z)_{ijk}e^{-u_{ijk}}$,
\begin{align}
(R_x)_{ijk}&=-\frac{u_{i+1,jk}-u_{i-1,jk}}{2\Delta x}-W_{ijk}\,\frac{z_{i+1,jk}-z_{i-1,jk}}{2\Delta x}+(L_x)_{ijk}e^{-u_{ijk}},\\
(R_z)_{ij,k+1/2}&=u_{ij,k+1}-u_{ijk}-\frac{\Delta s_k}{2}\big(W_{ijk}a_{ijk}+W_{ij,k+1}a_{ij,k+1}\big),
\end{align}
and $R_y$ like $R_x$. The vertical residual is the trapezoidal integral of Eq.~\eqref{eq:vert} over the interval, the same
rule that gives the heights in Eq.~\eqref{eq:zsum}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The iteration}\label{sec:iter}
\subsection{Gauss--Newton}
$\Phi$ depends non-linearly on the unknowns $v=(c,u)$ through the equation of state, the opacity in the heights, and the field
derivatives. Each step replaces the residual vector by its linear approximation, $\R(v+\delta v)\approx\R+\mathsf J\delta v$, and
minimises the resulting quadratic (Gauss--Newton, the least-squares form of Newton's method). Setting its gradient to zero gives
the normal equations
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\big(\mathsf J^{\!\top}\mathsf W\mathsf J+\mathsf E+\mu_{\rm LM}\,\mathsf D\big)\,\delta v=-\mathsf J^{\!\top}\mathsf W\,\R\;}
\label{eq:normal}
\end{equation}
with $\mathsf W$ the diagonal matrix of the weights of $\Phi$. $\mathsf E=10^{-2}\mathsf D$ is a small fixed damping and
$\mathsf D$ is diagonal: $\kappa_1^2\,\overline{\Delta z}_k\bar H_k^2$ for a pressure unknown and
$\kappa_1^2\sum_k\overline{\Delta z}_k\bar H_k^2\overline W_k^{\,2}$ for a column height, with $\kappa_1=2\pi/(N_x\Delta x)$ the
smallest horizontal wavenumber and $\overline W_k$ the layer median of $W$; this is one per cent of the horizontal stiffness of
the longest wave. The damping matters in particular for the uniform shift of all columns, which changes no residual
(Sect.~\ref{sec:det}).

\subsection{The exact linearisation}\label{sec:lin}
$\mathsf J$ is the exact derivative of the discrete residuals; it is never stored, only applied to vectors. Its ingredients are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Equation of state:} $\partial q/\partial u=q(\partial\ln\rho/\partial\ln P-1)$ from the table derivative, and
$\chi'=\partial\ln\chi/\partial\ln P$ by a central difference ($\pm10^{-4}$ in $u$). Hence
$\partial W/\partial u=g\,\partial q/\partial u+L_ze^{-u}$ and $\partial(Wa)/\partial u=a\,\partial W/\partial u-W\,a\,\chi'$.
\item \textbf{Heights:} from Eq.~\eqref{eq:zsum}, $\delta a=-a\chi'\delta u$, so
\begin{equation}
\delta z_{k_c}=\delta c,\qquad \delta z_{k+1}=\delta z_k+\frac{\Delta s_k}{2}\big(a_k\chi'_k\,\delta u_k+a_{k+1}\chi'_{k+1}\,\delta u_{k+1}\big),
\end{equation}
a cumulative sum down the column (with $\delta u_{k_c}=0$), and $\delta z=\delta c$ above the cut.
\item \textbf{Lorentz force through the heights:} a change of the heights changes the vertical spacing,
$\zeta_k=(\delta z_{k-1}-\delta z_{k+1})/(z_{k-1}-z_{k+1})$, which changes $\partial_zf$ by $-\zeta_k\,\partial_zf$; and it
changes the slopes by $\partial_x\delta z$, $\partial_y\delta z$, which changes $\partial_xf|_z$ by
$-(\partial_x\delta z)\,\partial_zf-(\partial_xz)\,\delta(\partial_zf)$. The resulting $\delta\Lv$ is linear in $\zeta$,
$\partial_x\delta z$ and $\partial_y\delta z$ and enters all three residuals ($R_x$, $R_y$ directly and through $W$; $R_z$
through $W$).
\item \textbf{Direct terms:} $-\partial_x\delta u$, $-W\,\partial_x\delta z$, $-(\partial_xz)\,\partial W/\partial u\,\delta u$
and $-L_xe^{-u}\delta u$ in $R_x$ (likewise $R_y$); $\delta u_{k+1}-\delta u_k-\tfrac{\Delta s}{2}\sum\partial(Wa)/\partial u\,\delta
u$ in $R_z$.
\end{enumerate}
Its transpose $\mathsf J^{\!\top}$ is applied by reversing each step (the cumulative sum becomes a sum from the bottom up). Both
have been checked against finite differences of the residuals.

\subsection{Solving the linear system}
Equation~\eqref{eq:normal} is symmetric and positive definite and is solved by the preconditioned conjugate-gradient method
(only matrix--vector products are needed), from $\delta v=0$, to a relative residual of $10^{-3}$ or at most 80 iterations.

\subsection{The preconditioner}
In a horizontally periodic box any function is a sum of horizontal waves, and a centred difference acts on a wave
$(k_x,k_y)$ as multiplication by $i\sin(k_x\Delta x)/\Delta x$. If all coefficients were the same throughout each level, the
operator would act on each wave separately as an $n\times n$ matrix
\begin{equation}
\mathsf A+\sigma^2\mathsf B,\qquad \sigma^2=\frac{\sin^2(k_x\Delta x)}{\Delta x^2}+\frac{\sin^2(k_y\Delta y)}{\Delta y^2},
\end{equation}
where $\mathsf A=\mathsf G_z^{\!\top}\mathsf W_z\mathsf G_z+\mathsf E+\mu_{\rm LM}\mathsf D$ comes from the vertical residuals
($\mathsf G_z$ the vertical Jacobian of one column) and $\mathsf B=\mathsf G_h^{\!\top}\mathsf W_h\mathsf G_h$ from the horizontal
ones, $\mathsf G_h=-\mathsf I_u-\overline W\,\mathsf S$ with $\mathsf S$ the cumulative height operator of item 2 above. The
preconditioner builds $\mathsf A$ and $\mathsf B$ from the layer medians of $a\chi'$, $\partial(Wa)/\partial u$ and $W$ (the
Lorentz-through-height and local Lorentz terms are left out). Rather than factorising $\mathsf A+\sigma^2\mathsf B$ for each of
the $N_xN_y$ waves, it is diagonalised once: with the Cholesky factor $\mathsf A=\mathsf L\mathsf L^{\!\top}$ and the eigenvectors
$\mathsf V$ and eigenvalues $\lambda_m\ge0$ of $\mathsf L^{-1}\mathsf B\mathsf L^{-\top}$, the matrix
$\mathsf Q=\mathsf V^{\!\top}\mathsf L^{-1}$ satisfies $\mathsf Q\mathsf A\mathsf Q^{\!\top}=\mathsf I$ and $\mathsf Q\mathsf
B\mathsf Q^{\!\top}=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda)$, so
\begin{equation}
(\mathsf A+\sigma^2\mathsf B)^{-1}=\mathsf Q^{\!\top}\,\mathrm{diag}\!\left(\frac{1}{1+\sigma^2\lambda_m}\right)\mathsf Q
\end{equation}
for every wave at once. Applying the preconditioner costs a horizontal Fourier transform, two products with the small matrix
$\mathsf Q$, and an inverse transform.

\subsection{Step control: Levenberg--Marquardt}
A pure Gauss--Newton step can be too ambitious where the problem is strongly non-linear. Three safeguards keep it reliable:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Whole-step scaling.} With $s_{\rm c}=\min\big(1,\ 2/\max|\delta u|,\ 300\,{\rm km}/\max|\delta c|\big)$, the
first trial is $\alpha=s_{\rm c}$: no pressure changes by more than a factor $e^2$ and no column moves by more than 300\,km
(\code{dc\_cap\_m}). Scaling the whole step keeps its direction, which is a descent direction of $\Phi$; clamping individual
components would not guarantee that.
\item \textbf{Backtracking:} $\alpha=s_{\rm c},s_{\rm c}/2,s_{\rm c}/4,s_{\rm c}/8$; the first trial that lowers $\Phi$ and
stays inside the equation-of-state table is accepted.
\item \textbf{Damping:} if none of the four trials is accepted, the damping is raised, $\mu_{\rm LM}\leftarrow\max(4\mu_{\rm
LM},1)$, and the step recomputed (at most 8 times). Large $\mu_{\rm LM}$ turns the step from the Gauss--Newton direction
toward the direction of steepest descent with a short length. After an accepted step $\mu_{\rm LM}$ is divided by 3 if the
full, unscaled step was taken ($\alpha=1$), kept if $\alpha\ge s_{\rm c}/2$, doubled otherwise, and set to 0 once below
$10^{-3}$. It starts at \code{lm\_mu0} = 0 (pure Gauss--Newton).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stopping rule}
After each accepted step the relative decrease $\Delta\Phi/\Phi=(\Phi_{\rm old}-\Phi_{\rm new})/\Phi_{\rm old}$ is computed. The
iteration stops when
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Delta\Phi/\Phi<\code{rtol}=10^{-4}$ on \code{n\_consec} = 3 consecutive steps (converged); or
\item the number of steps reaches \code{max\_steps} (100); or
\item no step is accepted even with the largest damping.
\end{itemize}
The stopping rule is on relative decreases of $\Phi$, which does not by itself prove that a minimum has been reached. As a
diagnostic, one further undamped Gauss--Newton step is computed at the returned state (but not taken), and its predicted
relative decrease of $\Phi$, the \textbf{Gauss--Newton decrement} $-\delta v^{\top}\mathsf J^{\top}\mathsf W\R/\Phi$, is
reported together with the gradient norm $\|\mathsf J^{\top}\mathsf W\R\|$ and the stop reason (converged, step limit,
or no descent). A small decrement means that the linear model sees little left to gain.
The criterion is on the objective only. A single small decrease is not enough, because $\Phi$ is nearly flat along the
heights of a few strong-field columns (Sect.~\ref{sec:det}) and an isolated small step can occur long before the minimum. The
largest height change of each step and the percentiles of $z(\tau=1)$ are logged but not used to stop.

\subsection{After the iteration}
All columns are shifted together so that the mean height of $\tau_{500}=1$ is zero (the gauge), and $\rho$ and $\chi$ are
evaluated on all levels. The returned fields are then checked: every state inside the table, all values finite, $P$, $\rho$,
$\chi$ positive, and the heights strictly decreasing with depth in every column (no folded column). The first failed check,
if any, is reported.

\section{What is determined, and what is not}\label{sec:det}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The global height.} Moving all columns up or down together changes no residual: the forces depend only on
differences and slopes of the heights. This offset is fixed by the gauge, a convention, not a measurement.
\item \textbf{The relative heights} of the columns are determined by the horizontal balance through the tilt term of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:horiz}, not by the alignment of the starting state, which serves only as the first guess. A few strong-field
columns are weakly constrained: their heights can change noticeably while $\Phi$ barely changes.
\item \textbf{The pressure level} comes from the $\tau$ scale (Sect.~\ref{sec:level}).
\item \textbf{Above the cut} nothing is solved; the levels keep the hydrostatic structure of the starting state.
\item \textbf{The magnetic field is not made consistent with the new geometry.} The field values on the $\tau$ levels are kept
while the levels move in space; $\nabla\cdot\B=0$ is not enforced for the field at its new positions. The Lorentz force is
computed from the field as it lies.
\end{itemize}

\section{What decides the heights: the Wilson depression}
Consider a column with a strong, nearly vertical field among weak-field neighbours. Its magnetic pressure $B^2/2\mu_0$ pushes
outward on the neighbours, so at the same height it can hold its own against them with less gas pressure. Less gas pressure
means lower density and lower opacity, so light escapes from deeper: the $\tau$ surfaces of this column lie lower. Horizontal
balance favours exactly this through the tilt term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:horiz}. The depression of strong-field regions, seen in
sunspots near the limb as the \textbf{Wilson depression}, is therefore expected to come out of the force balance rather than
being put in by hand, although for a given column the result also depends on its temperature structure and on how well the
compromise balances the forces there. Conversely, hot granules have a high opacity, and their $\tau$ surfaces tend to lie
higher.

\section{Summary of the algorithm}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Read $\ell_k$, $x$, $y$, $T$, $\B$. Compute the column-hydrostatic starting state in $\tau$ (top closure, Heun
integration with substeps resolving steep pressure rises, total-pressure alignment, gauge).
\item Choose the cut level nearest $\log_{10}\tau=-4$. Fix the levels above it relative to the cut level, and the cut-level
pressure. Unknowns: $c$ per column and $\ln P$ below the cut.
\item Compute the weights $\bar H_k$, $\overline{\Delta z}$ from the starting state; evaluate heights, $\Lv$, residuals and $\Phi$.
\item Repeat: build and diagonalise the layer-median preconditioner; solve the damped normal equations by preconditioned CG;
scale the step as a whole to the caps; backtrack; raise the damping if no trial lowers $\Phi$; accept and update the damping.
Stop after three consecutive steps with $\Delta\Phi/\Phi<10^{-4}$, after 100 steps, or when no step is accepted; report
the Gauss--Newton decrement at the result.
\item Gauge the mean height of $\tau_{500}=1$ to zero; compute $\rho$; check the fields; write $z$, $P$, $\rho$, $T$, $\B$ and $z(\tau=1)$.
\end{enumerate}
