## Findings, ordered by priority

### 1. High: finite-difference preconditioner probes can leave the barrier domain

The earlier review identifies a concrete defect in `HSEMHS/src/MHSStaggered.jl:683–722`. The default magnetic layer estimate evaluates gradients at fixed perturbations ±1e-4; the scalar b-bar estimate also uses central differences, unconditionally. A feasible base point does not ensure these probes are feasible. Outside the barrier domain, `objective!` returns Inf while omitting the offending face's barrier gradient. Differencing those gradients and taking an absolute value cannot provide valid local curvature.

The earlier review records a 4×4×9 reproduction with base slack 1e-6 sigma and positive b-bar probe displacement 1.19e-5 sigma. The positive probe has infinite objective; exact base-state GN curvature is approximately 1.142857145e17, while the finite-difference estimate is negative and its absolute value, 3.698800788e14, is about 309 times too small. Its reproducer is `HSEMHS/scripts/m5_bifrost/codex_m5f_review_checks.jl`. I inspected that reproducer and confirmed the defective code path in this consolidation; I could not rerun Julia here. These numerical values and the earlier 10/10 focused-test result are evidence recorded in the 19 October review, not new measurements today. The referenced output log is unavailable locally.

**Claude next action:** compute curvature from the accepted interior state using GN products or analytic derivatives, including b-bar. Selecting `:layer_gn` fixes only the magnetic layer probes; b-bar still needs repair. Instrument domain violations at saved checkpoints to measure their incidence. Retain spatial barrier curvature rather than just its layer average. For T mapping magnetic coordinates to normalized face residuals, the barrier Hessian contribution is `Tᵀ diag(h) T`, with `h_f = (2 lambda/N_B)(u_f²+c_f²)/(u_f²-c_f²)²`. Its diagonal uses squared stencil coefficients. Replace an existing barrier contribution rather than adding it twice. Add the near-boundary reproducer as an asserted regression. This defect supports a preconditioning hypothesis; it does not establish the cause of every cluster stall.

### 2. High: the Cauchy safeguard does not apply to the final bounded step

`HSEMHS/src/MHSJointGN.jl:162–177` compares the GN candidate with a pressure-projected Cauchy candidate before either is made face-feasible. Lines 196–215 then scale the selected candidate, potentially almost to zero, without comparing against a face-feasible Cauchy candidate. Thus the documented sufficient-decrease safeguard is lost in bounded mode. Diagnostics called `share_final` and the model split are also computed before this final scaling.

A simple quadratic illustrates the issue: minimize `-x-y + (x²+y²)/2` from zero with upper slack `1e-10` on x. Scaling the direction `(1,1)` by `0.99e-10` gives predicted decrease about `1.98e-10`; the feasible direction `(0,1)` gives `0.5`. Accurate local curvature alone does not remove this geometric issue. This is a mechanism for poor progress, not a demonstrated diagnosis of every reported collapse.

**Claude next action:** construct and compare candidates after applying all feasibility operations. At minimum, apply face truncation separately to GN and Cauchy candidates and recompute their model decreases before selection. For reliable progress near active faces, use a constraint-aware Cauchy/tangent direction or another method that can move along the active constraints; radial truncation of both directions alone does not guarantee that. Compute final diagnostics afterward. Add a small bounded quadratic regression with a nearly active face and a useful tangent direction.

### 3. Medium: PDHG lower-bound extraction uses the opposite sign from its multiplier update

`centre_bracket.py:128–129` uses the saddle convention `||c||∞ + lambdaᵀ(Ac-r)`. Its dual objective is `-lambdaᵀr`, subject to `||Aᵀlambda||₁ <= 1`. But `lower()` at lines 102–104 evaluates `+lambdaᵀr / ||Aᵀlambda||₁`. The latter is a valid weak lower bound for an arbitrary multiplier, so this does **not** create a false certificate. It systematically wastes the sign of a useful PDHG multiplier, however.

**Reproduced locally:** extracting the actual `prox_inf()` and running the same updates for scalar `A=1, r=1` gives `c=1, lambda=-1`. The known optimum is 1, the current reported bound is -1, and negating the multiplier gives 1. The reported negative LB is therefore not sufficient evidence that the dual iterate has made no useful progress.

**Claude next action:** evaluate the sign consistent with the update, or both signs using `abs(lambdaᵀr)/||Aᵀlambda||₁`. Initialize the bound with zero and the already computed necessary local bound (~2.705 in the prior review), retaining a corresponding certificate. Re-evaluate saved multipliers before rerunning PDHG. Test scalar and small LP cases with known optima. This finding does not invalidate the physical upper-bound witness.

### 5. Medium: the quantity logged as magnetic change also includes pressure change

`m5f_pilot.jl` sets `dBw = measured_change(pb, wx, snap_prev[])` and prints it as ΔB. But `MHSStaggered.jl:864–879` returns the maximum of relative pressure change and normalized magnetic change. Thus pressure may be printed again under the magnetic label. The earlier review's finding is confirmed directly from both functions.

**Claude next action:** calculate magnetic change separately, or pass current pressure together with the previous magnetic snapshot to isolate B, as the earlier comparison diagnostic does. Test a pressure-only change and a magnetic-only change. Retain the pressure nonconvergence conclusion, but do not interpret the current ΔB column as an independent magnetic convergence measure.

### 6. Medium: “anytime certified bracket” and verification need explicit numerical checks

`centre_bracket.py:91–101` ignores CG's `info` and does not check the projected equality residual. Final lines 143–146 print divergence but label maximum feasibility solely from UB. Intermediate saves contain no physical witness. `verify_field.py` prints metrics without enforcing finite values or divergence/acceptance tolerances.

Recomputing the centre residual from reconstructed B is the right check. An imperfect affine projection need not invalidate UB if the reconstructed physical field is independently divergence-free to an explicit tolerance. That condition should be checked, rather than inferred from CG or reconstruction. The report appropriately calls its result numerical rather than exact arithmetic; the script's “certified” wording is stronger than the implemented checks.

**Claude next action:** save and validate CG status, equality residual, reconstruction mismatch, finiteness and physical divergence; gate positive feasibility labels on those checks. Save the physical incumbent with every improved UB and use an external wall guard, because a report's projection can exceed the loop's wall budget. Describe the bracket as numerically verified unless rigorous rounding/error bounds are supplied. Add a small end-to-end known-field check plus an intentionally failed projection/invalid-field check.

### 7. Medium: new constraint code lacks automated regression coverage and API validation

No new tests under `HSEMHS/test` exercise the hinge/barrier or hard-bound path. The pilot's `check` mode prints derivative errors and symmetry/positivity but asserts no tolerance; it tests the barrier well inside its domain. Symmetry and positivity alone cannot establish the correct barrier Hessian magnitude.

`joint_gn_solve!` documents a feasible base but does not enforce it, validate `0 < bound_tau < 1`, or check the final magnetic violation before acceptance. The pilot checks its initial field, which protects that caller, but the solver API itself does not. `set_face_hinge!` also accepts negative lambda or invalid thresholds, undermining its convexity claim.

**Claude next action:** validate parameters and finite initial feasibility in the solver, and check final bounds within declared numerical tolerances. Add automated tests for both residual signs, nearly active faces, b-bar motion, invalid starts/parameters, and preservation of divergence and pressure-anchor constraints. Compare the isolated barrier Hessian product with finite differences of its gradient, including a controlled near-bound case. Exercise the new global minimum reduction with a limiting face on another MPI rank when the environment permits. An unchanged default path and a passing old suite do not validate the new opt-in behavior.

## Scientific interpretation and answers to the brief

1. **Observation contract:** yes, the released-data operator and coarse-grid representation-error model belong in the user's acceptance decision. The two particular witnesses' cross-residuals do not prove the face and centre admissible sets are disjoint. These fields are not established global minimizers for both criteria. Joint face-plus-centre linear bounds can be presented as a diagnostic intersection problem; they should not become a new acceptance requirement without scientific justification. If both residuals derive from the same observations, their errors are correlated; treating them as independent measurements double-counts information. Large face components hidden by averaging also motivate a nullspace/roughness audit of the centre witness before any MHS initialization.

2. **Centre tail:** a bounded fixed-support feasibility test using at most K largest residuals is a reasonable next diagnostic, after extracting the corrected saved dual bound. Preserve the maximum bound and reconstruct/verify the field. Failure for that support does not settle feasibility over all supports. Keep a data-only diagnostic separate from the user's decision to adopt that model.

3. **Bounded solver:** prefer a repaired constraint-aware step plus a spatially resolved barrier preconditioner and declared continuation schedule before another long run. The barrier gradient and exact convex curvature formulas in `MHSStaggered.jl` and `MHSGaussNewton.jl` are algebraically consistent. The current preconditioner attempts to include barrier curvature through probes, but can evaluate invalid outside-domain gradients and averages spatial variation away; “CG does not see the curvature” is inaccurate because the GN product explicitly includes it. Log local curvature/slack, CG residuals and post-truncation model decrease to test the conditioning explanation. Do not accept tau=0.99 as a robustness result. An augmented Lagrangian or active-set method is a possible later alternative, not yet justified as a necessary rewrite.

At fixed lambda=0.1, the objective remains barrier-modified. Reduce lambda only after an adequate inner solve and monitor the original constrained stationarity conditions. For this normalization, the implicit upper/lower bound multipliers each have slack product lambda/N_B; their total complementarity is 2 lambda. This is not a global optimality certificate for nonlinear MHS, and a fixed positive lambda does not establish the zero-barrier KKT limit.

4. **Plateau:** declare an engineering stagnation stop before the next experiment, alongside work and wall limits. Judge progress using force and constraint metrics as well as the original objective: its data/smoothness floor can hide force improvement. RMS 109 versus target 0.01 and large pressure changes establish nonconvergence, not infeasibility. Solver repair and a stop policy can both proceed; neither requires extending the fragile run. Save damping, radius, counters, reference settings and provenance for reproducible continuation.

The ADMM affine projection/box splitting is consistent with the stated minimum-norm QP. Primal/dual residuals and objective stabilization are useful stopping evidence but are not themselves a reported numerical KKT gap; remove that terminology from `data_qp.py` or compute stationarity/complementarity and an appropriate gap. Maintain the distinction between approximate QP optimality and independently checked field feasibility. There is still no new evidence here requiring a pressure prior.
